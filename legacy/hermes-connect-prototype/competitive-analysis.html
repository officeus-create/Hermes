Hermes Connect · competitive position · 2026-08-04

The incumbent for Ukrainian salons is still owned by the company that walked away from them.

Every major booking platform in this market is either Russian-linked (Altegio/YCLIENTS), feature-thin (appointer.ua), or not built for this market at all (the US platforms). None of them are simple. Hermes Connect can win on trust and on design — not by matching every feature, but by being the one calm, obviously-independent option.

Why "closed access to Ukrainian clients" checks outTrust gap

This isn't marketing spin — it's the documented ownership history of the market leader.

2022

YCLIENTS formally exits Ukraine shortly after the full-scale invasion. Local salons lose direct access to the platform they were running their business on.

2022

Altegio launches as the replacement — presented as an independent spin-off, built on licensed YCLIENTS source code, serving roughly 500 Ukrainian businesses today.

Dec 2023

VK (Russia) acquires 100% of the YCLIENTS group — the company whose code and technology Altegio is built on. Independent investigations describe Altegio as a Western-market rebrand of the same Russian company, not a clean break.

Now

Ukrainian salons are choosing between a platform with unresolved Russian ownership ties, or appointer.ua — a genuinely local product, but a dated, feature-thin one that still ships a Russian-language mode.

What "better" has to mean hereNot a feature race

Matching Altegio feature-for-feature is a multi-year project and mostly the wrong fight. Three things actually move a Ukrainian salon owner:

Trust

Visibly, verifiably not Russian-owned

Say it plainly on the pricing page, not just imply it. This is the one advantage no competitor here can copy without lying.

Design

Mangomint/GlossGenius-level simplicity, not Altegio/Vagaro density

Every review of the market leaders eventually says "powerful but cluttered." One clear screen per task, generous spacing, no modal-inside-modal — that's the whole design brief.

Language

Real multilingual, not a bolted-on switch

Hermes already maintains en/es/fr/ua/it/ru locale infrastructure on the main site — reuse it here instead of building translation from zero, and it becomes a genuine reach advantage over the US-only platforms.

Build orderRoadmap

What exists today: real accounts, profile + service/availability editing, and test bookings, all on Cloudflare D1. Ranked by what a salon owner would actually miss first.

P0

Make the core loop trustworthy

Nothing else matters if the basics feel fragile.

  • Client-facing booking confirmation screen with all details, not just a status string
  • Reschedule and cancel, not just create — right now a mistake can't be undone by the client
  • Multi-master/multi-service calendar view for the owner, not just one profile at a time
  • Basic reminder (email first — SMS needs a paid provider decision from the owner)
P1

Close the gap with paid competitors

The features every comparison article checks for.

  • No-show policy: optional deposit or cancellation-fee rule per service
  • Client history view for the master (repeat vs. new, last visit)
  • Public booking page per salon/master — a real link to share, like Booksy's profile page
  • Wire in the existing en/es/fr/ua/it/ru locale system from the main Hermes site
P2

Only once P0/P1 are proven with real salons

Real money and real infrastructure decisions — needs the owner in the room.

  • Payments/deposits processing (which provider, in which countries)
  • SMS provider integration (cost-per-message adds up fast at scale)
  • Inventory/stock tracking for product-heavy salons
  • Staff payroll/commission calculation
Deliberately not now: a client-discovery marketplace (Booksy/Fresha's model — needs real supply first), a native mobile app (the web version needs to prove itself first), multi-location franchise tooling, POS hardware integration.
