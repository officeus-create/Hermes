# Hermes Video Factory — DESIGN V4

Source of truth: `docs/design/HERMES_UNIFIED_BRAND_SYSTEM.md`.

## Style prompt

Hermes media should feel premium, operational, calm, precise, intelligent and unmistakably connected to a real system.

The video/media system follows **ONE HERMES / FOUR VISUAL ENGINES**. It must not create a fifth visual language or leak the style of a generation provider into Hermes.

Public scenes use Pearl/Paper/Cloud as the default reading environment. Focus Ink `#172033` is a bounded functional/media surface, not an automatic “serious work” background.

Motion supports hierarchy, causality and meaning. It must never imply live activity, performance, inventory, demand, pricing, automation or customer results that are not backed by evidence.

Direction accents identify context; they do not repaint the whole frame.

## Typography

- Display: Manrope, weight 700–800
- Body: Source Sans 3
- Metadata / technical labels: IBM Plex Mono

Rendered-video minimum targets:

- headline: 60px+
- body: 20px+
- important data labels: 16px+

Do not shrink critical copy to create artificial “technical” density.

## Four media engines

### Logistics — Spatial Operations

Show movement through space:

- aerial road/yard context;
- route progression;
- equipment;
- origin/destination;
- capacity/ETA/status only when real or explicitly sample;
- maps/data overlays where they communicate a real relationship.

Avoid generic truck stock montage as the only story.

### Marketing — Attention & Demand

Show movement of attention:

- content/search/social source;
- distribution;
- reach or interaction only when evidence-backed;
- website action;
- lead/evidence handoff.

Avoid random social-logo confetti, fake virality and invented result numbers.

### Technology — Digital Systems

Show movement of information:

- source/context;
- API/workflow;
- Website Factory;
- connectors;
- product UI;
- architecture;
- build/test/deploy/evidence states.

Code/terminal is explanatory, not decorative cyberpunk.

### Academy — Human Progress

Show movement of capability:

- learning;
- practice;
- review/feedback;
- skill progression;
- responsibility;
- human mentoring/review.

AI presenters may explain or localize, but should not replace human credibility.

## Signature-scene rule

Normally one primary visual story per asset or major website section.

Do not combine unrelated:

- parallax;
- particles;
- glow;
- 3D;
- cursor effects;
- multiple looping videos;
- terminal streams

simply to increase spectacle.

The viewer should understand what is moving and why.

## Motion

- entrance motion supports reading order;
- normal UI-derived motion: approximately 300–400ms;
- ambient branded motion: slow and bounded;
- no fast perpetual spins;
- no unrelated simultaneous animations competing for attention;
- interactive preview surfaces respect reduced motion;
- no “live” pulse unless the state is genuinely live or explicitly labeled sample/demo.

## Hermes Intelligence

Use one ownable Knot/Core/connected geometry.

It may visually respond to genuine states such as:

- idle;
- listening;
- processing;
- connected;
- action ready;
- success;
- attention/error.

Do not replace it with generic sparkle, robot-head, AI orb or provider-branded language.

## News / Insights / social media

For substantial public stories, media should derive from one canonical fact/source package.

Preferred flow:

`canonical Website/Insights source → approved media treatment → platform-native social derivative`

Do not render one piece of content identically across every channel.

- Telegram / Threads / X: stronger interface/status framing.
- Instagram / Facebook: preserve photography, people, video and emotional storytelling where useful.
- LinkedIn: restrained executive evidence.
- website: search-readable context, transcript/captions/metadata when relevant.

## Generated-media governance

HeyGen, Higgsfield, Runway and future media providers are replaceable production tools, not the system of record.

Before public use:

- verify source/asset rights;
- keep avatar/voice consent where applicable;
- label demo/sample content;
- remove provider chrome/branding unless intentionally part of a disclosed example;
- copy approved output into Hermes-controlled storage where licensing and the pipeline permit;
- retain enough provenance to reproduce or replace the asset;
- do not expose provider IDs as business truth.

If provider credits/access end, the brand system continues using approved Hermes assets, deterministic templates or a license-verified open/local workflow.

## Deterministic / open fallback

Preferred fallback order:

1. existing approved Hermes-owned media;
2. deterministic HyperFrames/GSAP templates;
3. CSS/SVG/native browser animation for website-derived scenes;
4. maintained open-source tooling or local generation with separately verified model/asset licensing;
5. static version if motion does not materially improve meaning.

No generated-media provider is required for the public website to load or convert.

## What NOT to do

- no generic decorative 3D trucks, laptops, cubes, spheres, graduation hats or AI planets as the primary identity;
- no arbitrary rainbow cards or department-specific mini design systems;
- no saturated full-canvas gradients by default;
- no fake metrics, fake load counts, fake customer results or unlabeled sample data;
- no provider-specific visual identity leaking into Hermes;
- no leaked variables, prompts, internal IDs or placeholder copy;
- no avatar/cloned voice without consent reference;
- no four nearly identical direction videos differing mainly by accent color;
- no full dark canvas merely to imply “technology”.

## Acceptance

A production Hermes media asset is ready only when:

- business truth is evidence-bounded;
- brand treatment matches V4;
- mobile/crop variants preserve the dominant message;
- text is readable;
- captions/transcript exist when appropriate;
- provider-specific style does not replace Hermes identity;
- a static/reduced-motion fallback exists where the asset participates in an interactive web experience.
