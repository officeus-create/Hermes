# Hermes Route-Demand Development — Communications Runbook

Date: 2026-08-11
Owner track: Issue #403

## 1. Core public story

Hermes is building a second demand channel around verified carrier capacity.

The operating idea is:

**Customer / Shipper / Dealer → Hermes → qualified transportation request → suitable carrier review**

Today, carriers still use existing freight sources and load boards. In parallel, Hermes can develop direct-freight relationships and route-aware public search visibility around verified equipment, preferred markets, and historical operating patterns.

The objective is to reduce total dependence on third-party load boards over time by creating more direct customer, shipper, and dealer inquiries in markets that participating carriers can realistically serve.

This is a development program, not a guarantee of rankings, traffic, direct loads, rates, dedicated lanes, or revenue.

## 5. SEO publishing gate for future route pages

Do not mass-publish city-to-city pages from raw route history.

A route page becomes publishable only when all of the following are true:

1. route evidence comes from reviewed historical operating data or another approved source;
2. no private customer, VIN, driver, exact live-location, rate-confirmation, or shipment-specific information is exposed;
3. the route represents a repeatable market pattern rather than one isolated load;
4. search intent is commercially relevant and not already owned by a stronger Hermes canonical page;
5. title, H1, content, internal links, and CTA are distinct enough to avoid doorway-page duplication;
6. customer CTA routes to the real transportation intake, not the fictional Load Board demo;
7. page makes no claim of guaranteed capacity, ranking, volume, rate, or dedicated service;
8. page is added to sitemap/IndexNow only after canonical/indexability review;
9. page is monitored in GSC/Bing for impressions, query fit, CTR, and cannibalization;
10. stale or unsupported route pages can be retired or consolidated after review.
