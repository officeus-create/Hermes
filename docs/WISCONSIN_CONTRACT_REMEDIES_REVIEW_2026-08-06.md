# Wisconsin Contract Remedies Review - Carrier Agreement

Status: owner-approved legal-risk review; not an attorney opinion and not production execution approval.

## Result

The agreement should not use an arbitrary fixed breach fine. Wisconsin courts enforce stipulated or liquidated damages only when the remedy is reasonable under the totality of the circumstances and is compensatory rather than punitive.

The safer Hermes remedy is therefore tied to the actual commercial bargain:

- unpaid contractual service fees on affected revenue;
- actual records when available;
- a reasonable, rebuttable revenue estimate if the Carrier withholds required records;
- no duplicate recovery of actual damages and liquidated damages for the same loss;
- no arbitrary fixed minimum fine;
- reasonable documented collection costs;
- an express substantially-prevailing-party attorney-fee clause;
- lawful equitable relief where money damages are inadequate.

## Wisconsin liquidated-damages rule

Wisconsin applies the Wassenaar reasonableness test. Relevant questions include:

1. whether the clause is intended to compensate or punish;
2. whether the harm was difficult to estimate when the agreement was made;
3. whether the stipulated amount reasonably forecasts the anticipated and actual harm.

Key authorities:

- Wassenaar v. Panos, 111 Wis. 2d 518, 331 N.W.2d 357 (1983).
- Westhaven Associates, Ltd. v. C.C. of Madison, Inc., 2002 WI App 230.
- Rainbow Country Rentals & Retail, Inc. v. Ameritech Publishing, Inc., 2005 WI 153.
- Browning-Ferris Industries of Wisconsin, Inc. v. Sundance Photo, Inc., Wisconsin Court of Appeals (stipulated damages grossly exceeding actual damages are unenforceable; a transaction-based formula was upheld where reasonably related to loss).

The owner-review text therefore uses liquidated damages equal to the unpaid selected fee on affected revenue. That formula directly tracks Hermes's expected compensation instead of imposing a punishment unrelated to the transaction.

## Conspicuous presentation

A material remedy should be visible before signature. Wisconsin's commercial-law definition of a conspicuous term asks whether a reasonable person should have noticed it. The fee choice, Protected Account period, weekly invoice terms, and Wisconsin liquidated-damages heading therefore remain visible and separately labeled rather than hidden in unreadable fine print.

## Late charge

The signed agreement expressly states a simple late charge at the lesser of 1.5% per month (18% annually) or the maximum lawful rate, without compounding. It is not introduced only by a later invoice.

Relevant authorities include:

- Ziolkowski Patent Solutions Group, SC v. Great Lakes Dart Manufacturing, Inc., 2011 WI App 11 (a service provider could not add 18% interest through invoices when the signed engagement agreement was silent).
- Mid-State Contracting, Inc. v. Superior Floor Co., Inc., 2002 WI App 257 (discussing an expressly written 18% invoice term in a UCC merchant transaction and Wis. Stat. § 138.04).

## Attorney fees

Wisconsin follows the American Rule, so attorney fees must be clearly authorized by contract or statute. The revised clause expressly allows the substantially prevailing party to recover reasonable fees and court costs in defined enforcement actions, subject to Wis. Stat. § 814.045.

Key authorities:

- Murray v. Roundhouse Marketing & Promotion, Inc., Wisconsin Court of Appeals.
- Wisconsin Seafood Co. v. Fisher, Wisconsin Court of Appeals.
- Maringer v. Burback, Wisconsin Court of Appeals (contractual fee provisions and Wis. Stat. § 814.045).

## Personnel / no-hire risk

The prior wording prohibited a Carrier from soliciting or hiring certain Hermes personnel. That wording was removed.

Heyde Companies, Inc. v. Dove Healthcare, LLC, 2002 WI 131, invalidated a business-to-business no-hire provision that burdened employees' mobility without their knowledge and consent. The revised Hermes clause now states that the Carrier is free to hire or contract with any person. It prohibits only using a person to divert Hermes accounts, misuse Hermes confidential information, or continue Hermes services in breach of written duties for the purpose of avoiding Hermes's fee.
