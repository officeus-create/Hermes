## Positioning

Hermes helps collect and review the information needed to coordinate an open vehicle move. Open transport is commonly used for standard vehicle moves, dealer inventory and multi-car capacity, but availability, pickup, delivery and price are not guaranteed before a qualified carrier accepts the request.

## Key factual boundaries

- Do not call open transport risk-free, damage-free, guaranteed, cheapest or always available.
- Do not present Hermes as owning a nationwide fleet unless documented.
- Do not promise a pickup date, delivery date or fixed rate before written confirmation.
- The carrier makes the final acceptance decision.
- Any route distance or duration is a preliminary planning estimate, not truck-specific navigation.

## Information to collect

- origin and destination city/state/ZIP;
- vehicle count;
- year, make, model and body type;
- operable/inoperable status;
- whether each vehicle rolls, steers and brakes;
- keys available;
- modifications, low clearance, oversized tires or added equipment;
- earliest pickup and delivery window;
- residential, dealer, auction, port or business access details;
- open transport preference;
- target price as a non-binding input;
- contact details and consent.

VIN and exact private addresses should be collected only in an approved private workflow when operationally required.

## Open versus enclosed comparison

Explain neutrally:

- open equipment generally carries vehicles exposed to normal weather and road conditions;
- enclosed equipment provides a covered trailer environment and may be considered for specialty requirements;
- equipment choice depends on vehicle characteristics, handling needs, lane, timing, carrier capacity and accepted commercial terms;
- neither format eliminates all operational risk.
