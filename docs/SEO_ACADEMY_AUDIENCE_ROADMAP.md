# Hermes SEO Academy Audience Roadmap

## Purpose

This document is the persistent SEO execution queue for Claude Code, Claude Web/Cowork, Codex, ChatGPT, and other approved specialist agents working on the Hermes website.

The owner is the only CEO and final business decision-maker. When the owner says to proceed "at your discretion," the active technical agent should continue through the next safe, bounded tasks in this roadmap without repeatedly asking whether to keep going. The existing approval gates in `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/AI_ROLES.md` still apply to merge, production deployment, DNS/Cloudflare/account settings, deletions, credentials, billing, and external communications.

## Primary business objective

The first SEO growth category is people who may become students, trainees, team members, managers, or future regional partners in the Hermes ecosystem.

The website should attract qualified people who want to learn and then work in one or more of these directions:

1. US Logistics
2. Marketing and SMM
3. Operations / COO / leadership
4. Sales and negotiation
5. Supporting roles that Hermes actually needs to hire and can train into paid work

The SEO system should not present education as an isolated generic course business. The positioning is:

- practical training tied to real business workflows;
- a path from learning to measurable work, responsibility, and possible employment or partnership;
- access to the Hermes ecosystem across Logistics, Marketing, Academy, and IT;
- global participation with location-specific landing pages where demand and hiring priorities justify them.

## Primary audience

### Audience priority 1

Ukrainians and Russian-speaking people worldwide whose first language is Ukrainian or Russian and whose English is strong enough for practical conversation, customer communication, sales, or US logistics work.

This audience may become clients or team members across all four Hermes directions:

- US logistics services and operations;
- marketing and SMM;
- Hermes Business Academy;
- IT and business automation.

### Audience priority 2

International candidates in target cities and countries who speak English plus a locally valuable second language and are interested in remote work, training, management, sales, logistics, marketing, or opening a future Hermes office.

### Candidate intent segments

Create separate search journeys for:

- beginners seeking a career change;
- students and people without prior experience;
- experienced salespeople moving into US logistics or marketing;
- dispatchers, carrier sales representatives, driver recruiters, brokers, and shipper/dealer managers;
- SMM specialists, content strategists, marketing consultants, and sales consultants;
- operations managers, team leads, future COOs, and regional directors;
- entrepreneurs who want to build a local office or agency with Hermes systems;
- multilingual professionals seeking remote US-facing work.

## Training and hiring directions to support with SEO

Prioritize pages and clusters for:

- US logistics training for beginners;
- dispatcher training;
- carrier sales training;
- driver recruiter training;

- organic programming and low-budget paid promotion;
- lead generation and sales funnels;
- scripts, hooks, content plans, and conversion journeys;

### Operations / COO / leadership learning paths

Prioritize pages and clusters for:

- operational director training;
- COO training from zero to managing a team;
- leadership and team management;
- hiring, delegation, KPI, reporting, and meeting systems;
- launching a department or regional office;

Build Ukrainian and Russian-language entry points first for global users, while preserving English pages for commercial and US-facing intent.

### International city priority

Avoid thin doorway pages. Every location page must have unique audience logic, local language or employment context, program fit, FAQ, and a clear next step.

## SEO architecture to build

### Hub pages

Create or strengthen these top-level hubs:

- Academy overview
- US Logistics Training
- Marketing and SMM Training
- COO and Operations Training
- Sales and Negotiation Training
- Careers and Training Opportunities
- International / Multilingual Programs

### Program pages

Each program page should explain:

- who it is for;
- what practical work the person will learn;
- realistic prerequisites;
- language and schedule expectations;
- training format and duration only when owner-approved and current;
- practice and mentor model;
- possible roles after demonstrated results;
- honest limitations and no unsupported income promises;
- application or consultation path.

### Role pages

Create dedicated role-intent pages where search intent differs substantially, for example:

- how to become a US logistics dispatcher;
- carrier sales training;
- driver recruiter training;
- freight broker assistant training;
- SMM consultant training;
- content strategist career path;
- operations manager training;
- COO training for international teams;
- sales manager training.

### Supporting content clusters

Build educational articles, checklists, comparisons, glossaries, and FAQ pages around each program. Every article should link to a relevant program or role page and should answer a real candidate question rather than exist only for keyword count.

## Conversion strategy

Primary conversion actions for the first SEO category:

- apply for training;
- request a consultation;
- choose a program;
- indicate country, city, languages, English level, experience, and goal;
- choose whether the goal is employment with Hermes, building an independent skill, or becoming a regional partner;
- enter the correct logistics, marketing, sales, or operations funnel.

Keep preview-first and privacy-safe form behavior unless the owner separately approves live delivery.

## Trust and claims policy

Use only owner-approved, verified claims. Do not publish unsupported guarantees about employment, income, office opening, view counts, client results, or training outcomes.

Where the business has real evidence, create evidence modules with:

- dated case context;
- source or internal verification status;
- what was actually achieved;
- what cannot be generalized;
- clear distinction between training opportunity and guaranteed result.

## Task queue for the active SEO agent

Work in small branches and PRs. Complete one phase, update `docs/AI_HANDOFF.md`, and then continue to the next safe phase when the owner has said to proceed at the agent's discretion.

### Phase 0 — Baseline and measurement

1. Confirm PR #3 is merged and production is healthy before starting implementation work from this roadmap.
2. Verify GA4 is working on production after deployment.
3. Verify Search Console ownership, sitemap submission, indexing status, and major coverage issues through connected tools.
4. Record current baselines: indexed pages, impressions, clicks, top queries, countries, languages, conversions if available, and Core Web Vitals.
5. Build a keyword and intent inventory organized by program, role, language, and geography.

### Phase 1 — Information architecture and gap audit

1. Audit current Academy, Careers, Marketing, Logistics, and localized pages against this roadmap.
2. Produce a route map showing existing pages, missing hubs, duplicate intent, and pages that should not be indexed.
3. Identify the minimum first release: one Academy hub plus four program hubs and the highest-value role pages.
4. Define internal linking from homepage, direction pages, careers, and localized pages.
5. Propose URL slugs, titles, descriptions, H1s, schema type, and conversion action for each planned page.

### Phase 2 — First-priority program pages

Implement in this order unless data shows a stronger order:

1. US Logistics Training
2. Marketing and SMM Training
3. COO / Operations Training
4. Sales and Negotiation Training
5. Careers and Training Opportunities

For every page, add tests and follow the repository's content/claim policies.

### Phase 3 — Role-intent pages

Start with roles that Hermes is actively recruiting or can train now. Suggested initial set:

1. Logistics Dispatcher
2. Carrier Sales Representative
3. Driver Recruiter
4. SMM Consultant / Content Strategist
5. Sales Consultant / Sales Manager
6. Operations Manager / COO
7. Regional Representative / Head of International Direction

Before implementing each page, confirm from the latest owner instructions and handoff that the role is still active.

### Phase 4 — Ukrainian and Russian audience entry points

1. Create strong localized Academy and program overview journeys, not direct word-for-word translations.
2. Write for Ukrainians and Russian-speaking people worldwide who can work in English.
3. Explain language expectations honestly.
4. Connect localized pages to English role/program pages where practical.
5. Add hreflang/canonical logic only after auditing the current localization architecture.

### Phase 5 — Location expansion

1. Select cities based on demand, hiring need, language fit, competition, and ability to provide unique content.
2. Start with a small pilot, such as Berlin plus one or two other owner-priority cities.
3. Do not mass-generate city pages.
4. Require unique local sections, local language context, realistic schedule/time-zone information, and a region-specific application route.
5. Measure indexation and engagement before scaling.

### Phase 6 — Content production system

1. Create topic briefs for each program cluster.
2. Publish answer-first, evidence-based articles and checklists.
3. Add FAQ schema only when the FAQ is visible and genuinely useful.
4. Link each article to one primary conversion page and relevant supporting content.
5. Create a content refresh schedule based on Search Console performance.
6. Do not automate mass publishing until quality gates, duplicate-content checks, and owner-approved claims are enforced.

### Phase 7 — Authority and off-site work

1. Build a list of legitimate directories, education listings, diaspora communities, professional associations, podcasts, and partnership opportunities.
2. Prioritize earned links, expert contributions, local partnerships, alumni/candidate stories, and useful free resources.
3. Avoid purchased spam links, automated comment links, PBNs, and fabricated reviews.
4. Track each off-site placement and its source in a dedicated log.

### Phase 8 — Continuous optimization

1. Review Search Console weekly and GA4 monthly during the growth phase.
2. Find high-impression, low-CTR queries and improve titles/descriptions carefully.
3. Find pages ranking 5-20 and strengthen content, links, and evidence.
4. Merge or remove cannibalizing pages through reviewed PRs.
5. Track applications by program, language, country, and role.
6. Update this roadmap when the owner changes hiring or training priorities.

## Delegation and token-efficiency rules

The active agent should preserve premium coding context for architecture, implementation, debugging, and review.

Delegate where useful:

- ChatGPT: coordination, explanations, GitHub documentation, research, source verification, task decomposition, and overflow implementation through connected tools.
- Claude Web/Cowork: live-site audit, browser-connected checks, SEO review, architecture, and implementation briefs.
- Claude Code: local code, shell, tests, commits, branch pushes, PRs, and browser automation on the Mac.
- Codex: primary implementation when available; continue from handoff rather than restarting.
- NotebookLM / Gemini: long-source synthesis, market and curriculum evidence organization.
- Kimi / Perplexity / other approved tools: bounded research or second opinions, with conclusions verified before implementation.

When a task is likely to consume substantial context, the active agent should create a compact delegation brief with:

- objective;
- exact source files or URLs;
- constraints;
- required output;
- verification criteria;
- where to write the result.

## Autonomous continuation rule

When the owner says "do everything at your discretion," "continue," or equivalent:

1. Read the source-of-truth files and latest handoff.
2. Check current branches, PRs, and CI.
3. Select the next unblocked item in this roadmap.
4. Create a feature branch.
5. Implement the smallest coherent slice.
6. Run required tests.
7. Commit, push, open a PR, and update the handoff.
8. Continue to the next safe slice only when it will not overlap an open branch or require an approval-gated action.

Do not repeatedly ask whether to continue ordinary branch-based work. Stop and ask only when:

- a business fact or public claim is missing;
- two materially different product strategies require owner judgment;
- an approval-gated action is necessary;
- another agent is actively editing the same files;
- tests reveal a risk that cannot be safely resolved within the task scope.

## Definition of success

The SEO program is succeeding when it produces qualified applications and consultations from the target audience, not merely more indexed pages.

Track:

- qualified organic applications;
- consultation requests;
- applications by program and role;
- language and country distribution;
- conversion rate from organic landing pages;
- indexed pages with impressions;
- non-branded clicks;
- position growth for program and role queries;
- candidate quality and downstream progression into training, work, or partnership.
