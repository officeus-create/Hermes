# Hermes Ecosystem Compounding Standard

Reviewed: 2026-08-03

This standard applies to every approved AI agent and human contributor working on the Hermes ecosystem. It governs website, SEO, content, analytics, data, automation, Academy, Logistics, IT, Marketing, CRM, and AI-platform work.

## 1. Primary operating principle

Do not treat a task as valuable only inside the section where it originated.

Every bounded change must be evaluated for low-cost opportunities to strengthen several of these outcomes at the same time:

- search visibility and query ownership;
- commercial conversion and qualified inquiry flow;
- expertise, trust, and evidence;
- internal linking and information architecture;
- the public knowledge hub;
- future automation and reusable components;
- Hermes AI products and operating tools;
- user usefulness and decision quality;
- privacy-safe data and measurement;
- reuse across Logistics, IT, Marketing, Academy, and future business units.

The objective is compounding value, not uncontrolled scope expansion.

## 2. Required opportunity scan

Before implementation, inspect the current commercial owner, related content, existing components, open PRs, analytics boundaries, and canonical data sources. Then evaluate the following dimensions.

### A. Commercial SEO and conversion

Check whether the bounded change can safely improve:

- distinct commercial or problem-solving query coverage;
- title, description, H1, schema, FAQ, or visible proof;
- a relevant CTA and qualified intake path;
- expectation-setting, trust, objections, or safety language;
- natural links to the canonical commercial owner;
- measurable funnel events without sending submitted values or PII to analytics.

Do not create a competing thin conversion page when an existing canonical owner already serves the intent.

### B. Knowledge hub

When the task produces durable knowledge, consider a reusable:

- article;
- guide;
- instruction;
- comparison;
- checklist;
- glossary entry;
- research note;
- methodology;
- benchmark or market report.

A supporting resource must own a distinct informational intent and naturally lead to the relevant commercial service or product.

### C. News and current-information integration

When current developments materially affect the topic, consider:

- a relevant news or update category;
- links from evergreen pages to current coverage;
- links from news back to durable guides and commercial owners;
- update dates, sources, and clear separation between facts, estimates, and opinion.

Do not add a generic news block merely to make a page look active. Current information must be relevant, maintainable, and sourced.

### D. Programmatic scaling

Evaluate whether the underlying structure could later support verified variations by:

- city or state;
- service;
- equipment or truck type;
- customer category;
- industry or niche;
- route or operating pattern;
- language or audience.

Programmatic potential is not permission to publish pages. New URL families require evidence of distinct intent, unique user value, canonical ownership, data quality, sitemap ownership, and tests. Never generate doorway pages or perform name replacement over a generic template.

### E. Internal linking

Every indexable page must strengthen the existing graph when relevant. Consider links to:

- canonical service owners;
- supporting resources;
- tools and calculators;
- FAQ and glossary entries;
- Academy programs;
- research and benchmark reports;
- related audience or workflow pages.

Links must help a user continue a real task. Do not add arbitrary keyword links or excessive repeated navigation.

### F. AI platform and automation

Assess whether the task exposes a reusable capability for:

- a calculator;
- document or checklist generator;
- validation or normalization;
- AI-assisted analysis;
- search and retrieval;
- workflow automation;
- preview and human approval;
- reusable structured output;
- future Hermes CRM, Hermes Connect, AI Dispatch, Carrier Intelligence, Academy, or reporting products.

Preview-first and human-controlled behavior remains the default. A public content task does not authorize a live integration, external write, credential use, billing change, or production automation.

### G. Data and measurement

Evaluate what structured information could improve the product or decision process, including fields such as ZIP, city, state, equipment class, carrier, broker, dealer, route, FMCSA status, USDOT, MC, VIN, price, and delivery time.

This is an opportunity assessment, not blanket permission to collect or publish data.

Before adding any field or dataset, define:

1. the user or business purpose;
2. public, private, personal, regulated, credential, or financial classification;
3. source and provenance;
4. consent or lawful handling requirement;
5. retention and deletion policy;
6. access boundary;
7. whether analytics must receive only a boolean, category, count, or status rather than the submitted value;
8. whether the field belongs in a public form, private workspace, approved connector, or not in the product at all.

Never place names, email addresses, phone numbers, submitted URLs, messages, MC/USDOT, VIN, routes, rates, budgets, credentials, contracts, or payment details in GA4, public URLs, public fixtures, screenshots, or GitHub examples.

### H. Future products

Evaluate whether the work creates a primitive for:

- Hermes CRM;
- Hermes Connect;
- Hermes Academy;
- AI Dispatch;
- Carrier Intelligence;
- dealer, broker, or shipper workflows;
- market and benchmark reports;
- website and SEO diagnostic products;
- reusable client or partner portals.

Record strong product opportunities in the issue or handoff. Implement them immediately only when they are bounded, low-risk, tested, and compatible with the current mission.

### I. Content reuse and distribution

Durable content should be structured so approved teams can later reuse it for:

- the website and knowledge hub;
- Facebook, Instagram, Threads, LinkedIn, and Telegram;
- short and long video;
- YouTube descriptions and scripts;
- newsletters and email education;
- Academy lessons and internal knowledge;
- sales enablement and onboarding.

Do not automatically publish or send content. Produce reusable source blocks, verified facts, suggested excerpts, and channel-safe summaries for an approved communication workflow.

### J. Reusable architecture

Prefer:

- shared data models;
- configuration-driven content;
- reusable components;
- typed schemas;
- modular forms and validation;
- central CTA and analytics contracts;
- composable layouts;
- unit and browser tests that protect shared behavior.

Do not create a generic abstraction when one concrete use exists and the abstraction would increase complexity. Reuse must be earned by a credible second use or a stable shared contract.

## 3. Immediate implementation gate

Implement an adjacent improvement in the same PR only when all of these are true:

- it supports the current user journey or accepted issue;
- it is small relative to the primary task;
- it does not change another team's files or active branch ownership;
- it does not create a new unapproved URL family, live integration, data store, external action, or legal claim;
- it does not require unverified business facts, testimonials, locations, prices, results, or identities;
- it does not introduce canonical conflict, keyword cannibalization, privacy exposure, or migration risk;
- it can be covered by the current verification plan;
- the PR remains reviewable and has one coherent objective.

If any condition fails, record the opportunity as a bounded follow-up instead of implementing it immediately.

## 4. Cross-ecosystem design rule

Any new function, article, tool, data structure, or page should be evaluated for reuse across Hermes Logistics, Hermes IT, Hermes Marketing, Hermes Business Academy, and future product lines.

Reuse does not mean mixing audiences or placing unrelated offers on one page. Preserve distinct commercial ownership and audience journeys while sharing safe components, schemas, validation, analytics contracts, content primitives, and knowledge structures.

## 5. Required completion scorecard

Every implementation handoff must report these fields:

- **Primary outcome:** what bounded problem was solved.
- **SEO:** query ownership, metadata, schema, crawl/index implications.
- **Conversion:** CTA, qualification, trust, or funnel improvement.
- **Knowledge:** durable guide, FAQ, checklist, glossary, or evidence created.
- **Internal linking:** pages strengthened and links added.
- **Scale:** safe programmatic or multi-audience potential identified.
- **AI/product:** reusable tool, data model, automation, or product primitive.
- **Data/privacy:** fields, event payloads, classifications, and protections.
- **Content reuse:** approved derivative formats made possible.
- **Architecture:** reusable components or contracts used or created.
- **Deferred opportunities:** valuable ideas intentionally left out and why.
- **Verification:** current-head build, static/unit checks, desktop/mobile browser tests, and screenshots when UI changed.

A handoff must never claim that all dimensions improved. It must accurately distinguish implemented value, evaluated-but-not-applicable dimensions, and deferred work.

## 6. Success definition

A successful Hermes change solves its assigned problem, strengthens the surrounding ecosystem where it is safe and economical, and leaves behind reusable knowledge or architecture without creating hidden risk, duplicated ownership, privacy exposure, or unreviewable scope.
