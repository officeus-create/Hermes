# London Sprint 2 — Lead Magnets, Sales, Content

This file completes actions 31–60 from issue #915.

## 31–40 Lead magnets and readiness checklists
31. **Website audit checklist:** mobile speed/usability, clear offer, service coverage, trust evidence, contact friction, CTA visibility, form path, analytics, indexability, title/H1/canonical consistency.
32. **Local SEO checklist:** real service area, GBP ownership, correct primary category, services, hours, NAP consistency, local landing relevance, review process, local links/citations, Search Console baseline.
33. **Google Business Profile checklist:** verified ownership, accurate name/category/address/service area, phone/site link, hours, services/products where appropriate, photos, review response process, UTM-tagged website link, duplicate-profile check.
34. **Meta Ads readiness checklist:** clear offer, audience hypothesis, landing page, conversion event, consent, creative assets, media budget, follow-up owner, response-time target, test duration.
35. **Social content readiness checklist:** target audience, 3–5 content pillars, proof inventory, spokesperson/creator availability, publishing cadence, CTA, DM routing, moderation owner, reuse plan, KPI definition.
36. **CRM automation readiness checklist:** lead sources, required fields, consent, stage definitions, owner assignment, response SLA, duplicate handling, follow-up rules, closed/lost reasons, reporting.
37. **US logistics role-fit checklist:** role preference, US-market understanding, schedule, English, phone comfort, analytical comfort, sales comfort, persistence, computer readiness, training time.
38. **Dispatcher readiness checklist:** geography basics, numbers/rates, listening, load detail capture, driver communication, urgency control, conflict handling, note-taking, repeated call practice, English readiness.
39. **B2 English readiness checklist:** understand normal-speed business speech, clarify missed details, state/repeat numbers and locations, ask follow-ups, summarise agreements, handle objections, use logistics vocabulary, write concise follow-up, sustain a call, accept correction.
40. **Academy application prep checklist:** city/time zone, languages/levels, current work schedule, logistics/sales background, equipment/internet, preferred track, English readiness, sample work/results if any, learning goal, availability for practice.

## 41–50 Outbound and sales scripts
41. **Cold email — website:** “Hi {{name}}, I reviewed {{business}} and noticed {{specific friction}} on the website path from search to enquiry. We build conversion-focused sites for local businesses and can send a short audit with the first 2–3 fixes. Worth sending?”
42. **Cold email — SEO:** “Hi {{name}}, I found {{specific local-search gap}} for {{business}}. We work on local SEO, website structure and measurement together rather than selling rankings. I can send a brief evidence-based audit if useful.”
43. **Cold email — SMM:** “Hi {{name}}, your social presence has usable material, but the path from content to enquiry looks unclear around {{specific observation}}. We can map a practical content-to-lead system and show the first changes in a short audit.”
44. **Cold email — Meta Ads:** “Hi {{name}}, before spending more on Meta, I’d check {{tracking/landing/follow-up issue}}. We manage campaigns only after the conversion path is ready. I can send a quick readiness review.”
45. **Cold DM — local business:** “Hi — I came across {{business}} while reviewing London {{niche}} businesses. I noticed one specific growth issue: {{observation}}. Want me to send a 2-minute audit here?”
46. **Walk-in opener:** “Hi, I’m not here to interrupt your day with a long sales pitch. We help local businesses improve websites, Google visibility and lead follow-up. I noticed {{specific issue}}. Who is the best person to send a short audit to?”
47. **Audit call discovery:** ask current lead sources; best customers; highest-value service; current website/Google/social owner; what is working; where leads are lost; response process; monthly capacity; current ad spend if any; next 90-day objective. End by restating the bottleneck before proposing scope.
48. **Website proposal follow-up:** “Just checking the proposal reached you. The key recommendation is still {{one bottleneck}}. If priorities changed, I can reduce the scope to the smallest useful first phase rather than forcing the full build.”
49. **Retainer proposal follow-up:** “The recurring plan makes sense only if {{recurring workload}} remains a priority. If not, we can convert this to a one-time project. If yes, the next step is confirming owner, measurement baseline and first 30-day test.”
50. **Academy applicant follow-up:** “Thanks for the application. The next step is readiness, not a job interview: we review your preferred role, English/communication level, schedule and ability to complete repeated practice. Training does not guarantee employment or income.”

## 51–60 Content/video engine briefs
Use 40–90 seconds by default; longer only when the topic needs demonstration. Every piece ends with one CTA: audit, guide, assessment or application.

51. **Website mistakes:** Hook: “A good-looking website can still lose every lead.” Show three failures: unclear offer, weak mobile CTA, no measurement. CTA: London website audit.
52. **Local SEO mistakes:** Hook: “Local SEO is not adding ‘London’ to every page.” Cover intent, real service area, GBP, useful pages and evidence. CTA: local SEO checklist.
53. **Google Maps visibility:** Hook: “Your map listing and your website must tell the same story.” Cover category, service, location truth, review process and landing-page consistency. CTA: GBP checklist.
54. **Meta Ads expectations:** Hook: “Ads cannot repair a broken follow-up process.” Cover tracking, landing page, creative, budget and lead response. CTA: Meta readiness review.
55. **SMM-to-lead system:** Hook: “Views are not a sales process.” Map content → profile → CTA → DM/form → CRM → response. CTA: content readiness checklist.
56. **US logistics career map:** Hook: “US logistics is not one job called dispatcher.” Explain foundations, dispatcher, planner, carrier sales, shipper/dealer sales and negotiation. CTA: role-fit assessment.
57. **Dispatcher vs load planner:** Hook: “Both touch freight, but they optimise different problems.” Compare communication intensity, planning depth, time pressure and skills. CTA: role-fit assessment.
58. **Carrier sales:** Hook: “Carrier sales is not reading a script faster.” Show qualification, discovery, value, objections, follow-up and contract handoff. CTA: Academy track page.
59. **B2 English for logistics:** Hook: “Grammar alone does not make you ready for a US logistics call.” Show numbers, locations, clarification, objections and summary. CTA: English readiness checklist.
60. **Academy process:** Hook: “Training is not watching videos.” Show orientation → guided work → recorded practice → feedback → repetition → assessment. CTA: Academy application/readiness page.
