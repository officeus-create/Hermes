## Purpose

Give Logistics Sales a consistent carrier path that works from the website and a shorter, easier link for SMS, WhatsApp, Telegram, or email.

The carrier should be able to:

1. understand the value before seeing legal detail;
2. confirm that the carrier keeps control of loads, drivers, equipment, safety, routes, compliance, and freight payments;
3. compare Essential, Pro, and Custom proposals;
4. review the agreement in plain language;
5. complete the carrier packet from a phone;
6. draw a signature and retain a PDF review/onboarding packet;
7. call Logistics Sales before the final step.

Neither URL may contain a carrier name, email, phone, MC/USDOT, plan selection, tracking identifier, or another private value.

## Conversion principle

Do not lead with penalties, collections, document hashes, attorney-review language, or technical execution status. Lead with the carrier’s practical questions:

- What will Hermes do?
- What remains under my control?
- Where does the freight money go?
- What percentage and scope am I considering?
- Can I ask a question before signing?

The legal and technical status must remain available, but it belongs after the plain-language summary or inside a clearly labeled document-status block.

## Recommended sales sequence

### 1. Confirm basic fit

Confirm only what is needed for the next step:

- carrier or fleet status;
- equipment type and capacity;
- authority and insurance readiness;
- current dispatch situation;
- operating area and availability;
- authorized business contact.

Do not request passwords, PINs, W-9 files, CDL images, bank information, VIN lists, gate codes, or private shipment records through SMS or a public URL.

### 2. Create agreement before sending the link

Use a calm transition:

> Based on what you told me, the next step is simply to review how the cooperation works and confirm that the responsibilities are clear. You keep control of every load and final operating decision.

### 3. Send the short signing link

Preferred SMS:

> Hermes Logistics: here is the private carrier link we discussed. You can review the working model, see what stays under your control, and complete the carrier packet from your phone: https://hermeslogisticsus.com/sign/

Shorter version:

> Here is the Hermes carrier agreement and packet link: https://hermeslogisticsus.com/sign/ Review it on your phone and call me before signing if anything is unclear.

Website or longer proposal handoff:

> I’m sending one private Hermes page where you can compare the support options, see what remains under your control, and prepare the carrier packet: https://hermeslogisticsus.com/carrier/

### 4. Give one clear instruction

> Open the link and choose the option that matches where you are: continue to the carrier packet, review the agreement first, or ask a question.

Do not tell the carrier to “just sign.” The action should be connected to understanding and confirmation.

### 5. Guide, do not pressure

Useful questions:

- Which part of dispatch takes the most time from you now?
- Which decisions do you want to keep personally?
- Do you need ordinary dispatch support or deeper route and customer-development work?
- Is the signer authorized to act for the carrier company?
- Which term should we clarify before the packet is completed?
- Does the percentage and service scope shown in the packet match what we discussed?

Do not use fake deadlines, false scarcity, guaranteed income, guaranteed loads, hidden conditions, or threatening language.

## Objection handling

### “I don’t want to lose control of my truck or loads.”

> You do not. Hermes can research, communicate, negotiate within your instructions, and coordinate paperwork, but your company makes the final decision on every load, driver, route, and operating issue.

### “Where does the broker payment go?”

> Freight payments continue going directly to your company or factoring company. Hermes receives only the service fee agreed for the work performed.

### “I need time to read it.”

> Absolutely. Use the agreement-review option, download the review copy, and call me with the exact clause you want explained. The packet will still be available when you are ready.

### “Why does the document say draft?”

> The current downloadable file is the review version. The final execution version must match the percentage and scope selected for your company. We do not want you signing a document that conflicts with the commercial terms you were offered.

### “I don’t want to send all my documents.”

> The public packet does not ask for passwords, bank information, W-9 files, CDL images, VIN lists, or private shipment records. Secure documents are handled separately only when needed.

## CTA hierarchy

1. `Continue to carrier packet` — strongest action after the working model is understood.
2. `Review the agreement first` — for a carrier who wants document clarity.
3. `Ask a question` — immediate human fallback.
4. `Review plans and support` — for an earlier-stage carrier who is still comparing value.
5. `Download review PDF` — supporting action, never the only next step.

## Visual hierarchy

- dark navy: trust, control, and serious business context;
- violet-to-magenta: the primary continuation action;
- green: control commitments, checks, and human support;
- warm gold: document status or “review carefully” information;
- red: security boundary or a genuine error only;
- four narrow rails: Logistics, Marketing, Academy, and IT Development visual continuity with the Hermes site.

Avoid using red around the main signature CTA, large warning blocks above the value summary, countdowns, urgency badges, or aggressive “sign now” copy.

## Minimum-data principle

The carrier should not be asked for every possible operational detail at the first step. The initial signing path should prioritize:

- legal company name;
- MC or USDOT;
- authorized signer name and title;
- business email and mobile/business phone;
- selected working model;
- equipment and operating summary;
- secure access-handoff method;
- electronic consent and signature.

Detailed address, private documents, credentials, banking data, and shipment records are not needed for the public handoff and should be collected later only through an approved secure process when genuinely required.
