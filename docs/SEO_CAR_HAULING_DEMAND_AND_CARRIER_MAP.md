# Hermes Car Hauling Demand and Carrier SEO Map

Status: working strategy document

Purpose: consolidate the strongest source-grounded car hauling, carrier, shipper, dealer, port, auction, and direct-demand opportunities already present in Hermes project materials. This document is not a claim that every route, market, or carrier is currently serviced. It is an implementation map for research, qualification, SEO content, CRM routing, and future page development.

## 1. Core operating model

Hermes should position the logistics offer around two connected departments:

1. Dispatch operations for current freight movement.
2. Direct freight development for building longer-term relationships with shippers, dealers, auctions, ports, manufacturers, warehouses, brokers, and direct customers.

The working growth sequence is:

**LOAD BOARDS FOR TODAY -> DIRECT SHIPPERS FOR TOMORROW -> INBOUND CUSTOMERS FOR LONG-TERM GROWTH**

The website must not promise direct freight, ranking, income, loads, or automatic eligibility. Qualification depends on authority, insurance, equipment, service record, communication, operational fit, route coverage, and current Hermes capacity.

## 5. Real inbound case pattern

A project source describes an anonymized inbound request involving a rare 1961 Maserati 3500 Vignale Spyder arriving from Puerto Rico, with pickup near JFK Airport and delivery to Wilmington, North Carolina.

Approved use:

- label as a real inbound request or example of direct inbound demand;
- explain qualification dimensions;
- use it to show how Hermes evaluates a specialty movement;
- do not identify the customer;
- do not publish phone, exact address, VIN, or private documents;
- do not say Hermes completed the move unless completion evidence exists.

Qualification dimensions:

- port and release details;
- customs status;
- arrival and release date;
- exact operational pickup and delivery details;
- year, make, model, VIN, and operability;
- open versus enclosed transport;
- luxury/classic vehicle experience;
- MC/DOT and insurance;
- port access and TWIC when required;
- special handling;
- storage and demurrage;
- accessorials;
- budget and rate negotiation.

### Supporting sources

- Central Dispatch exports supplied by the user;

- Telegram exports;

Supporting sources are clues, not final authority when they conflict with official FMCSA records.

## 8. Lead status rules

Recommended preview statuses:

- `add`
- `update`
- `hold`
- `reject`
- `needs_review`

Rules:

- use `update` only when the lead already exists;
- phone-only records remain `hold` or `needs_review` until enrichment;
- do not invent company, MC, DOT, city, state, or equipment;
- deduplicate by phone, MC, DOT, normalized company name, and DBA/legal-name match;
- identify `not interested`, `do not contact`, bad number, broker-only, and duplicate signals;
- box truck or general freight leads should not be routed into car hauling without explicit classification;
- official FMCSA data has priority over advertising directories;
- final CRM write should happen only after preview and review.

## 13. Conversion and analytics

Track only non-PII dimensions in general analytics.

Recommended events:

- service page viewed;
- route page viewed;
- equipment selected;
- pickup type selected;
- port/auction/dealer/private customer selected;
- open/enclosed preference selected;
- request form started;
- validation failed;
- request submitted;
- contact route clicked;
- carrier qualification started;
- carrier qualification completed.

Never send name, phone, email, MC/DOT, VIN, exact address, or free-text messages to general analytics.

## 14. Claim safety

Preferred language:

- may become eligible;
- subject to verification;
- designed to;
- based on current capacity;
- scope confirmed during onboarding;
- potential matching;
- example of direct inbound demand.

Avoid:

- guaranteed loads;
- guaranteed direct customers;
- guaranteed ranking;
- guaranteed delivery time;
- guaranteed income or weekly gross;
- top-paying without evidence;
- always available unless operationally verified;
- completed case study without proof.

## 15. Implementation order

### Phase A — source and capability verification

- identify current carrier capacity;
- confirm supported equipment;
- confirm supported geographies;
- identify existing direct-demand evidence;
- normalize route and carrier records;
- separate verified facts from hypotheses.

### Phase B — national commercial pages

- build the core car hauling hub;
- build dealer, auction, port, luxury/classic, enclosed, and capacity pages;
- implement schema, canonical, breadcrumbs, CTA, and analytics;
- add to sitemap;
- test all routes.

### Phase C — route and location pages

- publish only markets with verified operational relevance;
- use unique local and operational detail;
- connect every page to a broader service page;
- avoid city-swapped doorway content.

### Phase D — carrier and demand intelligence

- connect FMCSA enrichment;
- create source and last-updated fields;
- build carrier qualification previews;
- use route history to prioritize future pages;
- use Search Console data to expand pages where impressions and qualified demand appear.
