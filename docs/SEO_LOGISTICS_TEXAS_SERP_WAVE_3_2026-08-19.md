## Executive decision

**Texas dealer/auction vehicle-transport intent exists, but publication remains HOLD.**

Fresh sampled SERPs show commercial transport results for Texas, Dallas and Houston, including dealer-focused transport providers and auction marketplaces that surface transportation as part of dealer workflows. That establishes a real intent family. It does **not** establish that Hermes should create separate Texas, Dallas or Houston pages, because the current national Dealer Vehicle Transportation and Auction Vehicle Pickup owners already satisfy much of the same intent.

A geographic owner may move to build only if it adds material local value beyond inserting a city/state name into the national owner.

## What the SERP proves

1. Dealers search/browse transport solutions around dealer inventory, auction purchases, multi-vehicle movement and delivery.
2. Dallas and Houston have local commercial pages competing on dealer/fleet transport language.
3. Auction platforms themselves can own part of the transport journey, so a Hermes geographic page must offer value beyond generic "auction shipping in Dallas/Houston" copy.
4. Facility operating context can matter locally: pickup hours, release readiness, storage deadlines, appointment/access rules and inoperable/loading constraints.

## What the SERP does not prove

- Search volume or keyword difficulty.
- Hermes demand in Texas, Dallas or Houston.
- A reason to split one national Dealer owner into multiple city pages.
- A reason to split the national Auction owner by auction brand or city.
- Carrier capacity, rates, pickup timing or service economics.
- Hermes affiliation with ACV, ADESA, Copart, IAA, Manheim or any auction/facility.

Semrush API units are currently unavailable, so this wave intentionally does not claim quantitative volume/KD.

## Distinct-value requirements before a Texas build

A Texas statewide or metro owner must add at least several material local elements that cannot be represented cleanly on the national owner, for example:
- factual Texas/metro facility and access patterns relevant to the exact buyer intent;
- route/facility preparation guidance that differs materially from the national owner;
- a distinct local conversion context that the shared intake can preserve;
- verified service/support scope in the geography;
- local evidence or operational guidance that is permissioned and public;
- query evidence showing the geographic modifier represents a meaningful user need rather than simple keyword substitution.

A list of city names, auction brands, highways, ZIP codes or generic dealer claims is **not** distinct value.

## Cannibalization boundary

### National Dealer owner retains
- dealer-to-dealer inventory transport;
- dealer auction purchases;
- multi-unit dealer movement;
- recurring dealer workflow;
- open/enclosed options and shared vehicle-transport intake.

### National Auction owner retains
- auction/facility release readiness;
- storage deadline;
- operability/loading context;
- facility pickup coordination;
- auction-brand context without affiliation claims.

### A future Texas/DFW/Houston owner may exist only if
- the geographic query archetype is materially distinct;
- the page can provide local operational value without duplicating these national owners;
- canonical ownership is explicit;
- the shared intake preserves Texas/metro context;
- 390px funnel QA and 7/28-day measurement are defined before publication.

## Conversion-context requirement

Do not create a separate Texas backend. A future approved geographic page must reuse `/logistics/request-vehicle-transport/` and preserve only the minimum useful public context (role/request/geography/facility type) in URL/query parameters. VINs, gate passes, buyer numbers, release documents, credentials, payment data and other sensitive details stay out of public URLs and ordinary analytics.
