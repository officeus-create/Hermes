# Hermes Car Hauling SEO — Page Priority and Evidence Plan

Status: implementation planning
Owner: Hermes SEO / Logistics / IT
Working branch: `seo/academy-audience-roadmap`

## Purpose

Turn the broader car-hauling demand map into a safe publishing order. The goal is to create pages that can rank, convert, and be supported operationally without publishing thin location pages, unsupported claims, or unverified carrier availability.

## Publishing rules

Every permanent page must have:

- one clear search intent;
- one audience;
- unique H1, title, and meta description;
- substantive service copy;
- visible eligibility and scope;
- process steps;
- FAQ based on real customer questions;
- a specific CTA;
- internal links to related logistics paths;
- canonical URL;
- BreadcrumbList and valid Organization/Service schema where appropriate;
- inclusion in sitemap only after the page is production-ready;
- no guaranteed loads, income, direct customers, availability, ranking, or delivery time;
- no exact customer addresses, VINs, private phone numbers, or carrier credentials.

## Priority framework

### P0 — Build first

These pages match broad commercial demand, fit Hermes's existing service model, and do not require a narrow geographic proof claim.

### P1 — Build after P0

These pages require stronger operational detail, verified process capability, or one approved real example.

### P2 — Publish only after evidence is available

These pages depend on verified lanes, repeatable demand, port or auction capability, or confirmed carrier capacity.

# P2 pages — evidence-gated

Do not publish these merely because the keyword exists.

## Lane pages

Examples:

- `/routes/new-york-to-north-carolina-car-transport/`
- `/routes/florida-to-texas-car-transport/`
- `/routes/california-to-arizona-car-transport/`

Publish only when there is evidence of one or more of the following:

- repeated Hermes inquiries;
- repeated carrier movements;
- active verified carrier capacity;
- approved operational service coverage;
- meaningful unique content for both origin and destination markets.

A lane page must not be a city-name substitution template.

## Port-specific pages

Examples:

- `/locations/jfk-airport-vehicle-pickup/`
- `/locations/port-newark-vehicle-transport/`
- `/locations/port-of-miami-vehicle-pickup/`

Publish only after confirming:

- actual operational support;
- release and access process;
- nearby pickup geography;
- carrier requirements;
- unique FAQs and service constraints.

## Auction-specific market pages

Examples:

- auction pickup by metro area;
- auction-to-dealer lane pages;
- inoperable auction transport pages.

Do not imply affiliation with Copart, IAA, Manheim, ADESA, or another auction unless documented.

## Carrier-capacity pages

Examples:

- three-car hauler capacity in New York;
- enclosed carrier capacity in Florida;
- 7–10 car carrier capacity in the Midwest.

Publish only when capacity is verified and has a documented update date. Use language such as `capacity subject to current availability and carrier qualification`.

# Evidence matrix

| Evidence type | Can support | Cannot support alone |
|---|---|---|
| Signed Hermes service scope | Service process, fees, responsibilities | Demand volume, ranking, carrier availability |
| Verified intake form | Required customer/carrier fields | Service coverage or guaranteed response |
| FMCSA / SAFER / L&I | Authority, registration, safety and insurance-related records | Current willingness, lane availability, quality of service |
| Central Dispatch or internal export | Historical lead/capacity signals | Public claim without validation and permission |
| Internal call notes | Objections, recurring questions, demand signals | Public personal data or unverified company facts |
| Completed shipment evidence | Approved case study facts | General guaranteed performance |
| Inbound inquiry | Real demand example | Completed transport claim |
| Search Console | Queries, impressions, clicks, indexed pages | Market size or guaranteed commercial demand |
| GA4 | On-site behavior and conversions | Search ranking cause or offline revenue without attribution |

# Definition of done for a page

A page is ready for implementation review when:

- audience and search intent are singular and clear;
- all required business facts are confirmed;
- unsupported claims have been removed;
- title, meta description, H1, canonical, and schema plan are defined;
- CTA route is operational;
- intake fields match the service;
- related internal links are known;
- the page has enough unique content to stand alone;
- sitemap inclusion is planned;
- analytics events contain no PII;
- mobile and accessibility requirements are included;
- the page can be tested without external side effects.

A page is ready for production only after build, route, metadata, accessibility, internal-link, and sitemap checks pass and a human confirms the public business claims.
