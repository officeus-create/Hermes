# Hermes SEO — Port Vehicle Pickup SERP Validation

Date: 2026-08-19  
Scope: Google/Bing organic commercial SEO research only. No new production route is approved by this note.

## Decision

Port Vehicle Pickup remains a credible candidate for **one** dedicated commercial Logistics owner because current commercial search results distinguish port-to-door / port-to-dealer vehicle transport from ordinary door-to-door vehicle shipping.

The intent is operationally different enough to research further, but publication is held until query ownership and conversion mapping are complete.

## Search-intent archetype

Current commercial results commonly emphasize:

- vehicle pickup from a U.S. port or terminal;
- port-to-dealer / port-to-door inland movement;
- terminal release and pickup readiness;
- access credentials or escort context where applicable;
- paperwork/document readiness;
- storage deadlines or storage-fee exposure;
- carrier scheduling and equipment fit;
- delivery to dealer, warehouse, business, or customer destination.

This is not proof of Hermes demand volume, keyword difficulty, conversion rate, or ability to serve every port.

## Existing Hermes owner

Current production already has qualification owner:

`/paths/logistics/customers/port-pickup/`

It publicly supports:

- release and customs-status checklist;
- terminal/port/airport/warehouse access review;

That path establishes product/service truth for qualification. It does not automatically prove that a second commercial page should exist.

## Customs / regulatory boundary

U.S. Customs and Border Protection materials make clear that vehicle import entry and clearance involve CBP requirements and may involve a commercial customs broker. Hermes must not turn a transport-coordination page into an unsupported customs-brokerage claim.

Allowed wording:

- confirm or collect the **status** of customs/release documentation;
- ask whether the vehicle is released and ready for pickup;
- review port/terminal access information supplied by the customer or facility;
- coordinate inland carrier fit after qualification.

Not allowed without separate verified authority/capability:

- “Hermes clears customs”;
- “Hermes files your CBP entry”;
- “Hermes acts as your customs broker”;
- guaranteed customs release;
- legal advice about import eligibility;
- guaranteed TWIC access or escort;
- claims that Hermes operates a port, terminal, bonded yard or warehouse.

## Overlap review

Keep as qualification / decision owner. A commercial page must not duplicate the full path content.

### `/logistics/dealer-vehicle-transportation/`

Owns dealer inventory, dealer transfers and auction/dealer workflow. Port-to-dealer should cross-link here, not create a separate dealer-port permutation.

### `/logistics/auction-vehicle-pickup/`

Owns auction pickup. Auction-to-port or auction-to-export intent must not be silently absorbed into a port-import page.

### `/logistics/request-vehicle-transport/`

Remains the canonical conversion/intake endpoint. A Port Pickup page should not create a second backend/form.

## Publication gate

Move this candidate to build only if all are true:

1. query family remains distinct after current-owner overlap analysis;
2. title/H1 can target port vehicle pickup without implying ocean freight/customs brokerage;
3. page has unique operational value, not a thin rewrite of the path owner;
4. visible claims map to current public Hermes service truth;
5. direct intake can capture port/facility, release status, ready date, storage deadline, vehicle condition and destination safely;
6. no private release numbers, VINs, credentials or customs documents are placed in URL analytics;
7. internal links connect dealer, vehicle transport, relevant equipment and qualification owners;
8. Service/FAQ/Breadcrumb schema reflects only transport-review/coordination scope;
9. current SEO P0, thin-page, internal-link, privacy and mobile gates pass;
10. measurement owner is defined before publication.

## Evidence reviewed

- Current U.S. commercial search results for port vehicle shipping and port-to-dealer transport show dedicated port-pickup service archetypes.
- U.S. Customs and Border Protection, “Importing a Motor Vehicle,” confirms import entry/clearance documentation requirements and distinguishes commercial customs-broker activity from ordinary inland transportation.

Competitor pages are used only to classify search intent. Their capabilities, prices, coverage, response times, testimonials and performance claims are not Hermes facts.
