# Hermes SEO — Texas Vehicle Transport SERP Validation

Date: 2026-08-19  
Scope: Google/Bing organic commercial SEO research only. This document does not approve a Texas, Dallas, Houston, Austin, San Antonio, auction-facility, or dealer page.

## Decision summary

Texas remains the first non-Wisconsin Logistics market for deeper validation, but the SERP is not one homogeneous "Texas car shipping" market. Current results separate at least five useful intent families:

1. statewide consumer/commercial car shipping;
2. dealer inventory and dealer-to-dealer transport;
3. auction pickup and auction-to-dealer movement;
4. multi-vehicle / fleet / recurring inventory movement;
5. metro-level dealer and auction intent, especially Dallas–Fort Worth and Houston.

Research decision: validate these families separately before choosing a canonical Texas owner. Do not create one broad state page and then mechanically clone it across metros.

## Current SERP observations

### Statewide vehicle transport

Current Texas car-shipping results commonly combine statewide service with Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, Austin and San Antonio, plus personal vehicles, dealer inventory, fleets, open/enclosed equipment and route-specific capacity language.

Implication: a future statewide owner would need a distinct role beyond repeating generic city pages. It should explain Texas-wide route review, equipment fit, state-to-state and within-state movement, and the handoff to more specific dealer/auction workflows.

### Dealer inventory transport

Dealer-focused results emphasize:
- dealer trades and lot-to-lot inventory transfers;
- single-unit and multi-unit moves;
- auction pickups;
- recurring lanes;
- release information and vehicle condition;
- dealership/fleet workflow rather than consumer relocation copy.

Implication: Hermes already has a national canonical dealer owner at `/logistics/dealer-vehicle-transportation/`. Texas research should first test whether state/metro demand deserves a supporting local owner instead of creating a second page competing with the national owner.

### Auction pickup

Auction-focused results repeatedly reference Manheim, Copart and IAA context, release readiness, gate/facility requirements, storage deadlines and auction-to-dealer movement.

Official Manheim Texas inventory shows multiple auction nodes including Dallas, Dallas–Fort Worth/Euless, Plano and San Antonio, with explicit transport/gate operations. This supports auction infrastructure as a market-prioritization signal, not keyword-volume proof.

Copart's public pickup guidance confirms that vehicle buyers must arrange pickup/delivery and that storage timing matters, reinforcing release/storage readiness as a real workflow topic. It does not establish Hermes search demand or a right to publish facility-specific pages.

Implication: `/logistics/auction-vehicle-pickup/` remains the national commercial owner. Texas/Dallas facility language should be tested first as supporting content or a metro owner only if query evidence shows distinct intent.

### Multi-vehicle / recurring inventory movement

Current commercial results include dealer batches, full loads, fleet moves, rental/corporate vehicle programs and recurring dealer lanes.

Implication: this intent may map to existing Hermes owners such as dealer transport and multi-car transport rather than a new Texas page. Query ownership must be resolved before publication.

### Dallas–Fort Worth

Dallas SERPs contain dedicated dealer/fleet vehicle-transport pages, not only generic Texas car-shipping pages. Manheim also has Dallas and Dallas–Fort Worth nodes with published gate/transport hours.

Research priority: Dallas–Fort Worth is the first metro candidate for query-level validation.

### Houston

Houston results include dedicated dealer-trade/auction transport providers and Texas-regional car hauling. This indicates a separate commercial ecosystem, but current evidence is qualitative only.

Research priority: retain Houston as the second metro candidate after Dallas–Fort Worth.

## Evidence boundary

This validation establishes SERP archetypes and real auction/dealer workflow context. It does **not** establish:
- monthly search volume;
- keyword difficulty;
- conversion rate;
- Hermes carrier capacity in Texas;
- same-day pickup capability;
- customer count;
- dealer partnerships;
- auction affiliation;
- pricing;
- ranking opportunity.

Semrush quantitative validation remains blocked until API units are available. No volume/KD numbers should be invented or inferred from these SERPs.

## Next gate

Before any Texas URL moves from `research` to `approved_for_build`, require:
1. query set and intent classification;
2. existing-owner overlap / cannibalization review;
3. distinct value proposition supported by Hermes service truth;
4. direct conversion path;
5. internal-link plan;
6. sitemap owner;
7. measurement plan;
8. mobile/content QA;
9. no unsupported Texas capacity, customer, auction or timing claims.
